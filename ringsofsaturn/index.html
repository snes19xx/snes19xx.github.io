Reflections

I read W.G. Sebald's The Rings of Saturn last year and it left quite an impression on me, and my appreciation for it has slowly accumulated like sediment settling at the bottom of still water, so that it surfaces unexpectedly, the way certain memories do, in the middle of some unrelated afternoon. At this distance, now, I think about how Sebald managed to make of his own melancholy a kind of optical instrument, a lens ground from grief and erudition through which the wreckage of history becomes not overwhelming but strangely luminous, the way that Saturn's rings are themselves composed of debris, of ice and dust and the remnants of things that were once whole, orbiting that cold and distant ball of hydrogen and helium, and slowly, imperceptibly, falling away into nothing. And I think this is what the book is, really-- a debris field in slow and beautiful rotation around some unnamed loss that the narrator circles but never quite names.
